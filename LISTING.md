## Short description

Writes client proposals and quotes from a brief and rate card: the problem in the client's words, an honest scope with exclusions, three priced options, timeline, terms, and internal objection notes. Never invents rates, case studies, or guarantees.

## Long description

A proposal is a pricing decision disguised as a document. Most tools help you sound impressive; this one helps you get chosen.

From a brief, a rate card, and a deadline, it produces:

- **A diagnosis first.** The client's problem in their words, the measurable outcome, and what they are really buying.
- **A scope with teeth.** Inclusions with acceptance criteria, explicit exclusions, client responsibilities, and change control. The awkward assumptions get surfaced before the kickoff call, not after.
- **Three honest options.** Essential, Recommended, Premium — built from your rates, with the delta in one line per tier and the recommended option explained against the client's stated goal.
- **A real timeline.** Phases with durations and dates that add up, plus the client dependencies that can move them.
- **Terms that protect the seller.** Deposit and milestones, revision limits, IP on payment, termination notice, validity window.
- **Internal objection notes.** The three questions the client will ask — price, timeline, "we could do it ourselves" — with two-sentence responses grounded in the scope.

Then it self-reviews: every number matches the pricing table, dates are consistent, no placeholder survives, and no invented proof slips in.

Ships with the pricing and scoping reference (anchoring, deposits, discount discipline, scope-creep traps, discovery questions) and a complete worked example: a gym relaunch brief turned into a $6,500 / $9,800 / $14,500 proposal.
